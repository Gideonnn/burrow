Kaarten in Hedwig

Some mail is really a thing: a meeting, a login code, a parcel, a bill, a flight. For those, Hedwig shows a card (kaart) with the few facts that matter and the one action you came for, such as Deelnemen with a countdown. Automated mail can show the card instead of the message; the original is always one click away.

3Above the mail, or instead of it

Replacing a mail is a strong move, so it is earned, not the default.

Instead of the mail (in plaats van) only when all three hold

  • The mail is automated: a template, a noreply sender or List-Unsubscribe. Mail a person wrote is never replaced.
  • The card comes from structure or rules (iCalendar, schema.org, a rule, a template you confirmed), not from a model reading prose.
  • The card covers the mail: for an invite, the body holds nothing besides the meeting boilerplate. A note from the organiser shows as a quote in the card.

The original is always there

  • A dashed bar under the card: Origineel bericht tonen, key O. It opens in place, below the card, with the remote image bar as today.
  • The card menu (⋯) has Bij deze afzender altijd het origineel and Altijd de kaart; Instellingen › Hedwig › Kaarten has Automatisch, Altijd boven de mail and Uit.
  • Reply, forward, search and the phone see the real mail; the card is only how Hedwig on the Mac shows it.

4Every state

Time drives most cards. The countdown ticks once a minute, and once a second only in the last fifteen minutes before a meeting and the last ten of a code.

5In the list

A card adds one quiet line to its row with the time-bound fact, so the inbox shows what is next without opening anything. The line turns the card's colour when it needs you now, and a meeting gets a small Deelnemen button right in the row. A code row never shows the code.

Vandaag, and later

  • Vandaag sits on top of Alle inboxen and each own inbox: today's meetings and calendar items, today's parcels and bills due within a week, one line each, sorted by time. A meeting gets its countdown and Deelnemen; a click opens the mail, or Agenda for an item without mail. It folds away, and is gone when empty.
  • A notification one minute before a meeting with Deelnemen on it, also from the menu bar.
  • Hand-offs when the apps from the design system exist: Pakket to Errol, Reis to Knight Bus, Factuur to Gringotts, Afspraak to the Weasley Clock. The card stays Hedwig's small version.

6Where the data comes from, and what keeps it safe

Cards follow the facts: cheapest and most certain source first, and a card is derived from the mail, so it is as untrusted as the mail.

1 · StructureiCalendar and schema.orgParsed on Dobby for every stored message, no model. Exact times with time zone, the meeting URL, the order and track numbers. Most invites, airlines, shops and carriers send this.
2 · RulesCodes and expiryThe code rules already find the code; one more pattern reads "geldig tot" or "10 minuten". The code card is built only on the Mac app's request and never stored or sent to a cloud model.
3 · TemplatesHaiku, once per templateFor a template without structure, Haiku names which facts the template carries (delivery window, amount). Values come from each message's own facts.
4 · WinkyThe rest, locallyWinky's facts answer gets an optional card object. A model card is always shown above the mail, marked Gelezen door Winky.

Buttons

  • A button only opens a URL that is literally in the mail; the server checks it. The domain is printed on the button.
  • Deelnemen only for known meeting hosts (Teams, Zoom, Meet, Webex, Whereby). Any other link is Open link with its domain.
  • Ja, Misschien or Nee sends an iCalendar reply from your mailbox, only when you click it in the card. Nothing answers an invite on its own: no auto reply, no bot.
  • No pay button, ever. The bill card copies IBAN and reference and opens the existing factuur verwerken task.

Trust

  • Every card footer says where it came from: Uit de uitnodiging, Uit de gegevens in de mail, Herkend door de regels, Gelezen door Winky.
  • A bill whose IBAN differs from earlier bills of the same sender turns red and asks you to call them first. An invite from someone you never mailed says so.
  • Cards are sensitive like facts: IBANs and codes are redacted for cloud bots, and a facts-only key never gets a code or bill card.

7Calendars, before Burrow has its own

Burrow will get its own calendar later. Until then cards use the calendars the Mac already knows, read through EventKit with one permission prompt, so iCloud, Google and Exchange all count without new provider logins on the server.

Free or not

An invite shows Je agenda is vrij or Overlapt met Lunch met Bram before you answer, and Staat in je agenda after.

Vandaag

Calendar items without a mail stand in the strip next to the mail ones, with Deelnemen when the item has a meeting link.

In agenda

A trip, or an invite you got as a plain mail, goes into a calendar of your choice in one click; the item links back to the mail.

The server already keeps every invite it parses (UID, times, link, your answer). That store is the seed of Burrow's own calendar; when the calendar exists it replaces EventKit as the source and the cards stay the same.
